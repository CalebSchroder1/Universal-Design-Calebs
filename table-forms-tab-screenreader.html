Jabuticaba: The Tree That Fruits on Its Trunk

Last reviewed 26 October 2026

Overview

The jabuticaba (Plinia cauliflora) is a slow-growing tree in the myrtle family, native to southeastern Brazil. Its most unusual trait is cauliflory: the flowers and fruit sprout directly from the trunk and main branches rather than from the twig tips, so a mature tree can look as if it is covered in dark grapes.

Each fruit is roughly the size of a large grape. It has a thick, purple-black skin, a soft white jelly-like pulp, and one to four seeds. The flavor is often compared to a muscat grape with a slight tartness near the skin.

Growing and harvest

A tree grown from seed can take well over a decade to bear fruit, which is one reason jabuticaba is rarely sold outside its home region. Grafted trees fruit sooner. Under good conditions a tree may flower and fruit several times a year.

Why it is hard to find abroad

The fruit ferments quickly after picking, usually within a few days, so it rarely survives long-distance shipping.

Traditional uses

Most people eat jabuticaba fresh, squeezing the pulp out of the skin and spitting out the seeds. The skin is rich in anthocyanins, the pigments that give the fruit its deep color, and is often cooked into preserves.
